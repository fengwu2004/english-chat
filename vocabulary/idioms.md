# Common Idioms and Conventional Expressions

Created: 2026-10-16. Source: the learner-approved list in this conversation.

## Scope and ordering

119 unique expressions, with Chinese meanings and reference examples. This
includes conventional expressions and semi-transparent phrases as well as
figurative idioms. The ordering represents approximate frequency-informed
learning priority for American conversation and work; it is not a measured
frequency ranking or a direct transcription of a corpus frequency table.

Background: [Liu (2003), spoken American English idioms](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.2307/3588217).
The study informs the frequency-based approach, not the exact order here.

The previous 120-row chat list repeated “easier said than done” at 45 and 60.
Keep it once as I045; former items 61–120 are now I060–I119. The first thirty
items retain their order. Stable IDs identify targets, not frequency ranks.

## Learning plan

- Initial stage: I001–I030, six batches of five.
- Later stages: I031–I040, I041–I050, and so on, ending with I111–I119.
- Later stages remain deferred until the current stage passes its completion
  checks under [the idiom specification](../openspec/specs/idiom-learning/spec.md).
- Current status and all attempt evidence live in [the idiom log](../progress/idiom-log.md).
- These examples are reference material, not learner attempts or model answers
  to display before a retrieval exercise. Use different contexts in exercises.
- Do not move catalogue items into the phrasal-verb pools.
